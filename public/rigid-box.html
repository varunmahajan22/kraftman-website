Magnetic closure · Book style

How a magnetic book-style rigid box is made

  1. Greyboard, cut and V-grooved

    Dense greyboard is die-cut to the tray blank and V-grooved along every fold, so the walls stand at a crisp right angle instead of a rounded bend.

  2. Tray erected and corner-stayed

    The four walls fold up and each corner is taped, locking the tray square before any paper touches it.

  3. Magnets set in the front wall

    Two magnets are seated in the front wall. Once the box is wrapped they are invisible — you only feel them.

  4. Wrapped and turned in

    The printed wrap is glued, the tray is spotted onto it, and the paper is drawn up each wall and turned in over the top edge.

  5. Case made, magnets in the flap

    The cover is built flat like a book case: base, spine, lid and flap boards laid on the wrap with hinge gaps between them. Matching magnets drop into the flap, then the edges are turned in.

  6. Lined

    Lining paper covers the inside of the case and the tray, hiding every board edge and every magnet.

  7. Tray meets case

    The wrapped tray is glued onto the base panel of the case. Two parts become one box.

  8. The magnetic close

    Spine up, lid over, flap down. The magnets find each other and the box shuts with a soft snap.
